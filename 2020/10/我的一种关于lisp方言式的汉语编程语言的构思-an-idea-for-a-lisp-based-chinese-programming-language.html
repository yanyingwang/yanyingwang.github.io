缘由

从小学到中学阶段对写作的感兴趣所引发出来的对汉语的语言的特征感知，到工作之后作为一个程序员从事编程工作对编程语言特征的感知，所引发了我的思考进而写过一篇《由汉语编程所想到的》的文章零零碎碎的去阐述对中文编程，即是以汉语作为编程语言的元语言来进行编程，这件事情的意义的一些粗略看法。

汉语的优势

当今世界所存在的众多自然语言中，以英语这样的表音系统进而构建出来的语言占据大多数，其如西班牙语，德语，法语，日语，韩语，皆是如此。而在此外，中文汉语独以字形来表意，发音和表意两套系统却基本分离，这是中文汉语之所以可以和众多其他语言区别出来的典型特征。我也认为，得益于此，使得汉语在5000年的语言发展中摒除了不同时代的发音差异所带来的因素干扰，长远而有效的在表意这个层次上面发展的比其他语言都更为完善。

效益与成本

又因为汉语的这种独特性，当我们要讨论「为什么中文作为编程语言的元语言，和是否其他语言都没有取代英文作为编程语言的元语言」的时候就会明白，是因为汉语相较于其他语言在取代英语作为元语言上，有非常大的比较优势。也就是说，汉语取代英文作为编程语言的元语言的时候，所带来的的综合效益，明显的高于其他语言的取代。

但不管是用什么语言取代英语作为编程的元语言，所面临的首要问题都是成本问题，数以几十年的以英语作为编程语言元语言的积累和以之为基础的上层构造的积累，使得这种替换极为困难甚至说不可能被完成。但这并不代表这不能操作或者不值得操作。相反，从更长远的意义上综合来说，这恰恰是值得投资以来抵御风险因素，或者是应被列为某种尖端领域被政府投资鼓励来引导被发展的。

当今的现状

现在市面上充斥着的汉语编程语言，基本上是两类：一类试图以汉语的语法，来构造全新的编程语言；另一类试图以某种已经存在的英语作为元语言的编程语言为基础，进行关键字翻译，而保持原本的编程语法不变。

我的构思

编程语言语法的拓展，使得编程语言更加亲近自然语言。但是横览当今，编程语言始终是一种具有非常固定规则的编程程式而已，其在自由度上远无法企及自然语言比如有松散语法的英语，就更不用说汉语的这种以单字自由语素组建出来的语言了（以及古诗词对仗等底蕴单元造就的多样性所能另单数复数等等变化规律对仗）。

目前的编程语言中，语法极其简单的只有lisp，极其适合作为汉语编程的语法。那么剩下的就只是如何利用汉语博大精深的表意特性，来丰富化lisp语言的各种函数名而已。

我曾经想写一个「二十四节气」的封装，其用Racket语言来实现，即是想中文化各种命名。再加上Racket语言本身的自我定位就是「创造设计编程语言的编程语言」，WIKI上说：It is designed to be a platform for programming language design and implementation。

所以可以以Racket语言作为元语言，逐步的深入翻译其各种函数名，一方面逐步的深入到底层翻译，另一方面以汉语为中心构造上层建筑探索自身语言特色，综合性的来推进整个语言的进化和发展。

Background

From my interest in writing during primary and secondary school, I developed an awareness of the distinctive characteristics of Chinese as a language. Later, after entering the workforce as a programmer, I became increasingly aware of the characteristics of programming languages. These observations prompted me to reflect on the subject, eventually leading me to write an article titled Reflections on Programming in Chinese, in which I explored, in a somewhat fragmented manner, some preliminary thoughts on the significance of Chinese programming—that is, programming with Chinese as the metalanguage of a programming language.

The Advantages of Chinese

Among the many natural languages in the world today, most belong to language systems built around phonetic writing, such as English. Spanish, German, French, Japanese, and Korean are also examples of this general tendency. Chinese, by contrast, is distinctive in that its written characters convey meaning primarily through their forms, while the systems of pronunciation and meaning are largely independent of one another. This is one of the defining characteristics that sets Chinese apart from many other languages.

I also believe that this characteristic has enabled Chinese, over the course of its 5,000-year history, to develop its capacity for conveying meaning in a more sustained and effective manner than other languages, by insulating it from the disruptive influence of pronunciation differences across historical periods.

Benefits and Costs

It is precisely because of this distinctive nature of Chinese that, when we consider why Chinese has a comparative advantage as a metalanguage for programming, and why other languages have not similarly displaced English in this role, the answer becomes clear: Chinese has a substantial comparative advantage over other languages when it comes to replacing English as the metalanguage of programming. In other words, the overall benefits of replacing English with Chinese in this role would be significantly greater than those of replacing it with other languages.

However, regardless of which language we choose to replace English as the metalanguage of programming, the foremost obstacle is cost. Decades of accumulated experience with English as the metalanguage of programming, together with the layers of infrastructure and abstractions built upon it, make such a transition extremely difficult, perhaps even seemingly impossible.

Yet this does not mean that such a transition is infeasible or not worth pursuing. On the contrary, when considered from a broader, long-term perspective, this is precisely the kind of undertaking worth investing in as a means of mitigating potential risks. It could also be regarded as a frontier area deserving government investment and support to help guide its development.

The Current Landscape

Chinese programming languages currently available on the market generally fall into two categories. One attempts to construct entirely new programming languages based on Chinese grammar. The other takes an existing programming language whose metalanguage is English, translates its keywords into Chinese, and leaves the original programming syntax unchanged.

My Approach

Extending the syntax of programming languages can make them more accessible and more closely aligned with natural language. However, despite the various developments in programming languages to date, programming still relies on highly rigid syntactic rules. In terms of flexibility, programming languages remain far removed from natural languages such as English, whose grammar allows considerable flexibility, let alone Chinese, which allows words to be formed freely from individual characters that function as relatively independent morphemes. The rich linguistic traditions underlying Chinese, including the parallel structures found in classical Chinese poetry, further contribute to its expressive diversity, allowing patterns of correspondence and variation that go well beyond simple grammatical distinctions such as singular and plural.

Among existing programming languages, Lisp stands out for its exceptionally simple syntax, making it particularly well suited to serving as the syntactic foundation for programming in Chinese. The remaining challenge, then, is to make full use of the richness and depth of Chinese as a meaning-bearing system to develop a diverse and expressive vocabulary of function names for Lisp.

I once considered writing a wrapper for the Twenty-Four Solar Terms, implemented in Racket, with the specific aim of Chinese-ifying its various names.

Moreover, Racket positions itself as a language for creating and designing programming languages. As Wikipedia puts it: It is designed to be a platform for programming language design and implementation.

This suggests an approach in which Racket serves as the metalanguage: we could progressively translate its function names into Chinese, working our way down toward the underlying implementation while simultaneously building a higher-level structure centered on Chinese to explore the distinctive characteristics of the language itself. In this way, we could advance the evolution and development of the language as a whole from both directions.
